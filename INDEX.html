Chapter 1 • Preparation

BEFORE AN EARTHQUAKE

📖 Story: Preparing in San Juan de Lurigancho

Click words to view in Vocabulary

In San Juan de Lurigancho, earthquakes can happen at any time. Students at I.E. 0148 "Maestro Víctor Raúl Haya de la Torre" know that preparation is the key to safety.

Before an earthquake, we should prepare an emergency kit at home and school. This backpack should contain bottles of water, a flashlight, non-perishable food, and a first aid kit.

At school, all students should identify the safe area and know the clear evacuation route. We should always keep hallways and doors free of obstacles. We shouldn't place heavy objects on high shelves, and we shouldn't block the exits.

Finally, we should participate seriously in every school drill and discuss a family plan with our parents.

Chapter 2 • Action During Tremors

DURING AN EARTHQUAKE

📖 Story: Classroom Safety in Action

Click words to view in Vocabulary

It is Tuesday morning at school. Suddenly, the ground begins to shake violently. It is an earthquake!

First, students should stay calm. Do not panic or shout. Students should drop to their knees, drop under their desks, and protect their head and neck with their arms.

We should listen carefully to the teacher’s instructions. We shouldn't run out of the classroom while the ground is shaking. We shouldn't push our classmates or shout. Also, we shouldn't stand near large windows or heavy glass cupboards that could break.

Chapter 3 • Post-Earthquake Protocol

AFTER AN EARTHQUAKE

📖 Story: Safe Evacuation & Meeting Point

Click words to view in Vocabulary

When the shaking stops, the emergency is not over yet. Students should remain calm and wait for their teacher's signal to evacuate.

Students should follow the designated evacuation route and walk orderly to the safe meeting point in the courtyard. We should stay together with our class group so teachers can count all students. If someone needs help or is injured, report it immediately.

We shouldn't return to the building without official authorization from emergency authorities.

Grammar Focus: First Conditional

• If we follow the safety rules, we will be safer.

• If students run, they will put themselves at risk.

• If everyone helps, we will protect our school community.

EXERCISE 6 – CORRECT THE MISTAKES

Error Correction

Rewrite each sentence correctly in the box.

1. Incorrect: "We should to stay calm."

Explanation: Modal verbs like 'should' are followed by base verb without 'to'.

2. Incorrect: "Students shouldn't runs."

Explanation: Use base verb 'run', without 's'.

3. Incorrect: "If we will follow the rules, we will be safer."

Explanation: Use present simple after 'If' (follow), not 'will follow'.

4. Incorrect: "If it rain, there will be floods."

Explanation: Third-person singular 'it' requires 'rains'.

5. Incorrect: "You should listens to your teacher."

Explanation: Use base verb 'listen' after 'should'.

Unit 7 Grammar Structures Explanation

✓

SHOULD

Use: Give advice and good recommendations.

Subject + should + base verb
  • • We should stay calm.
  • • Students should follow safety plans.
  • • You should prepare an emergency kit.
✗

SHOULDN’T

Use: Express actions that are NOT recommended.

Subject + shouldn’t + base verb
  • • We shouldn't run during tremors.
  • • Students shouldn't push.
  • • You shouldn't stand near windows.
➜

FIRST CONDITIONAL

Use: Possible real situation & future result.

If + present simple, WILL + base verb
  • • If we follow rules, we will be safer.
  • • If students run, they will put themselves at risk.
  • • If we prepare, we will be ready.
